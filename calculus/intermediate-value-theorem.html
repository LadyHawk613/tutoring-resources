Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

Step-by-Step Proof Guide for AP Calculus AB

Step 1: State Continuity

Before applying the Intermediate Value Theorem, you must verify that the function is continuous on a closed interval.

What to write:

Because f(x) = x³ - 5x² + 7x - 9 is a polynomial function, it is continuous for all real numbers.

Step 2: Test Endpoints to Trap the Target Value

Find two values a and b such that the target output (100) falls strictly between f(a) and f(b).

  • Test x = 5:
    f(5) = (5)³ - 5(5)² + 7(5) - 9 = 26

  • Test x = 8:
    f(8) = (8)³ - 5(8)² + 7(8) - 9 = 239

Since 26 ≤ 100 ≤ 239, our target value of 100 sits cleanly between the two outputs.

Step 3: Write the Final IVT Conclusion Statement

Connect your findings using the formal requirements of the theorem to secure full credit on your test.

Conclusion: Since f(x) is continuous on the closed interval [5, 8], and 100 is a value between f(5) = 26 and f(8) = 239, by the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists at least one real number a in the open interval (5, 8) such that f(a) = 100.

Step 4: Visualizing the Theorem

Because the curve is continuous and goes from a low point (26) to a high point (239), it must cross every horizontal line in between—including y = 100!
